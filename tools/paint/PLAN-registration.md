## What the VIP page taught us (the rules)

**Sean's taste**
- Ana's world, not a template: her Greek village, sunflowers, bougainvillea pink, sunflower yellow, on a white or
  paper base with flair in shapes, never whole yellow backgrounds. Textures made in code, subtle, one per section.
- Premium UI, nothing that reads "made by Claude". Long reads in sans; serif for headlines and pulled lines.
- No Canva, no image generators: the paint engine (`tools/paint`) for drawings, code for interfaces.
- Real things where proof is needed (screens, numbers, her own photos); painted metaphors where the point is a
  feeling (the door, the chair, the sun).
- Apps people know are copied exactly and themed in her brand (the Gmail, the WhatsApp, the Zoom Q&A): realism is
  what makes people picture themselves inside.

**Jason Fladlien**
- Show, don't tell: every claim gets a picture of the real thing, revealed as it is said.
- One focal object per section; the biggest words are the most important; cut what doesn't persuade.
- A call to action after every section, worded for that section.
- Curiosity: show enough of the real thing to prove it exists and lock the rest ("shown live on Day 2").

**Motion**
- Real animation, never slide or fade: each picture acts out a small story (a pencil writes, a chat plays, a chair
  scoots, a list fills), on arcs, with anticipation and a settle. It plays when it can be seen, and plays again if
  you scroll back past it. Idle loops stay small. Everything still for reduced motion.
- Nothing may move while its own card is still fading in (the chair lesson); CSS never eases what GSAP moves.

**Skimming**
- The key line of each section is bold and swept in yellow as it arrives; one pulled line between sections.

**Chrome**
- One floating sticky header with the warning or event, the dates and a CTA; every CTA carries her sunflower,
  which spins on hover.
