# Revised workshop agenda

People read you. What makes them pay you?

For three days, I’ll take you inside the decisions behind my newsletter income, including the part of my approach I’ve changed this year. You’ll see what I got wrong, what I do differently now, and what that could mean for your own work.

## Day 1 · The paid opportunity hiding in what you already know.

My first workshop sold two copies. I knew my subject. I still missed what would make people want to buy. On Tuesday, I’ll show you what I’d look at differently now.

- What AI changed in the route from what you know to what you earn—and why I’d approach it differently if I were starting today.
- The difference between a topic readers enjoy and something they’ll consider paying for. It’s easy to mistake one for the other.
- Why the thing you dismiss as “too obvious” may deserve another look, even when there’s plenty of free information about it.

By the end: An offer idea worth testing, and a clearer sense of who would want it.

Next: why even a good idea can leave people saying “lovely” instead of buying.

## Day 2 · Give people a reason to pay you—without making yourself cheaper.

Let’s face it, a lower price can feel safer. But it doesn’t settle the question your reader is asking: “Is this worth paying for?” On Wednesday, we’ll get into what makes the answer yes.

- Why people can love your writing and still pass on what you sell—and why changing the price may not fix it.
- How I decide what to charge when the work feels easy to me, but would make a real difference to someone else.
- The four words I used when a client asked for my best price. They said yes.

By the end: An offer and a starting price you can explain without apologising for them.

Tomorrow: why a post with far fewer likes made more money than one people loved.

## Day 3 · Get beyond the likes. Give your readers a reason to buy.

One of my posts got 771 likes and made nothing. Another got 82 likes and made $120. Same writer. Very different result. On Thursday, I’ll show you what the likes didn’t tell me.

- The detail that made the difference between those two posts. I’ll put them in front of you so you can see it for yourself.
- Why I chose just 300 readers for my first ask, even though I had a much bigger list.
- What an invitation to buy can sound like when you don’t want to become a different person to sell.

By the end: A plan for approaching your first potential buyers, with your invitation written.

We’ll work on yours while we’re together, so it doesn’t become another thing you mean to do later.
